01

The real colours in an image

Drop a photo or a screenshot and get the colours actually in it — not a guess, not an average. Then send any one of them straight into the palette ramp.

USE IT WHEN A client sends a photo and says “make it match this” — and there is no brand file.

04

A type scale that actually holds

Pick a base size and a ratio. It writes the whole scale, checks each step still passes WCAG at its size, and hands you the CSS.

USE IT WHEN You are setting up type for a real project and do not want to eyeball eight sizes.

05

Will this hold up when it prints?

Clip a crop from the web, set the size it will be printed at, and see the DPI you actually have. Under 300 and you can see the difference on paper.

USE IT WHEN A client sends a photo from their phone and wants it on a banner.

02

Print bleed calculator

Finished size and bleed in, document size out — plus the safe area and the pixel count at your export resolution.

Bleed is extra artwork past the trim so a 1 mm cutting slip doesn't leave a white edge — 3 mm is standard.

Safe margin is how far text stays from the cut. DPI is the export resolution; 300 is print, 150 is a big banner.

USE IT WHEN Anything is going to a printer. This is the number behind most reprints.

03

Contrast checker (WCAG)

Type two colours and get the ratio. 4.5:1 is the line for body text, 3:1 for large text.

Text is the colour of the words, background is what sits behind them. Swap if you typed them the wrong way round.

USE IT WHEN Before you hand over a palette — a grey that looks fine on your monitor can disappear on a phone in daylight.

    Palette from one colour

    One colour in, a full ramp out. Contrast printed on every swatch, and the whole thing exports as CSS, JSON or Tailwind.

    50 is the lightest tint, 900 the darkest shade, base is your original colour. The two numbers on each swatch are its contrast against white and against black.

    USE IT WHEN A client gives you one hex and no brand guidelines.

    Drag anywhere in here — left to right is hue, up and down is saturation.

    Tap any swatch to copy its hex.

    Export the whole ramp
    08

    Can anyone actually read this?

    Text on a photo is the one thing nobody checks. Drop an image, type the headline, and this measures the contrast against the pixels actually behind the letters — then tells you the smallest scrim that would save it.

    USE IT WHEN You are setting type over a photo and the client will read it on a phone in a taxi.

      What font is this, really?

      Paste a font stack and see which face your machine actually renders — measured, not guessed.

      USE IT WHEN You are about to tell a client “it will look like this”. Their machine is not yours.

      Green = resolved here · grey = fell through. Every line is drawn in the face it names, so a fallback that looks wrong is a fallback that is wrong.
